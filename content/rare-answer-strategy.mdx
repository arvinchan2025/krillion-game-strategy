A useful Krillion strategy is to move beyond the first few answers that come to mind while keeping every guess relevant to the prompt.

The framework below is general trivia advice. It is not a disclosed formula from the game's creator and cannot guarantee a particular rarity score.

## Step 1: Lock down the prompt

Underline the key noun or category and note every restriction. Ask yourself:

- What exactly is being requested?
- Does the prompt specify a place, time period, type or attribute?
- Would a broad answer satisfy the wording, or is a more specific answer needed?

A clever answer that does not fit the prompt is not a good answer.

## Step 2: Empty the obvious-answer bucket

Write down the first three to five examples you would expect most people to think of. This clears the obvious candidates from your working memory and makes it easier to explore other directions.

## Step 3: Switch knowledge domains

Use a deliberate category rotation:

- **Nature:** unusual animals, plants, habitats and scientific names.
- **Geography:** islands, remote regions, local place names and landmarks.
- **History:** historical objects, figures, events and older terminology.
- **Culture:** folklore, literature, niche sports and regional traditions.
- **Specialist vocabulary:** technical names, taxonomy and industry terms.

Do not force a category if it does not fit the prompt.

## Step 4: Look for aliases

The official FAQ notes that answer matching can account for aliases. It gives an example of a fish with an unusual alternate name that can resemble another phrase.

This is a reminder that names are not always one-to-one. When learning a subject, note common names, scientific names, regional names and accepted synonyms.

## Step 5: Apply a relevance check

Before submitting, test the candidate:

1. Is it actually in the requested category?
2. Can you explain why it fits?
3. Is the spelling or name reasonably standard?
4. Is it a synonym or alias rather than an unrelated near-match?

## Step 6: Avoid “obscure for obscure’s sake”

The creator’s FAQ says the Krillion selection considers fun factor as well as rarity. A very obscure candidate is not automatically the best answer.

The best habit is to combine relevance, breadth and curiosity. Build a shortlist of defensible answers rather than chasing the strangest word you can find.

## A repeatable 30-second brainstorm

For practice, spend a short fixed interval on each prompt:

- First 5 seconds: identify the exact category.
- Next 5 seconds: list obvious examples.
- Next 10 seconds: rotate through two or three knowledge domains.
- Final 10 seconds: check relevance, spelling and alternate names.

This is a personal practice exercise, not an official Krillion timer or game rule.
